# Tianjin Jianbing Guozi (煎饼馃子)

## Description

A thin mung bean crepe cooked with an egg, brushed with savory sauces, and folded around a fried dough stick. It's the classic grab-and-go breakfast of Tianjin, a port city in northern China, where it was quick food for dockworkers and traders.

**Historical source:** Jianbing pancakes go back much further in neighboring Shandong province, and legend credits them to the Three Kingdoms era, but the Tianjin version has its own identity: the batter is mung bean flour instead of wheat or coarse grains. The name *jianbing guozi* first appears in print in a "Tiny Restaurant in Tianjin" supplement of the Tianjin *Ta Kung Pao* newspaper on 20 November 1933, and a 1942 *Jinjin Monthly* article describes grinding mung beans into batter. In 2017 the skill of making jianbing guozi was added to Tianjin's municipal intangible cultural heritage list. ([Wikipedia: Jianbing guozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianbing_guozi))

### Ingredients:
- [ ] 1 cup (120 g) mung bean flour
- [ ] 1/2 cup (60 g) all-purpose flour
- [ ] 1 1/4 cups (300 ml) water, plus more if needed
- [ ] 1/4 tsp salt
- [ ] 1/4 tsp five-spice powder (optional)
- [ ] 4 large eggs
- [ ] 4 youtiao (Chinese fried dough sticks), store-bought, or 4 large fried wonton crackers
- [ ] 4 tbsp sweet bean sauce (tianmianjiang)
- [ ] 2 cubes fermented bean curd (furu), mashed with 1 tsp of its brine
- [ ] Chili sauce, to taste (optional)
- [ ] 2 scallions, finely chopped
- [ ] 1 tbsp toasted black or white sesame seeds (optional)
- [ ] Neutral oil for the pan

### Steps for preparation:

1. Whisk the mung bean flour, all-purpose flour, salt, and five-spice powder in a bowl. Slowly whisk in the water until the batter is smooth and runny, about as thin as heavy cream. Let it rest for 10 minutes.
2. If you are using store-bought youtiao, warm them in a 350°F (175°C) oven or air fryer for 3 to 4 minutes so they are crisp.
3. In a small bowl, stir the sweet bean sauce and mashed fermented bean curd together.
4. Heat a large nonstick skillet (at least 11 inches) over medium-low heat and wipe it with a thin film of oil.
5. Pour in about 1/2 cup of batter and immediately tilt and swirl the pan (or spread with a spatula) into a thin, even circle.
6. When the edges look dry (about 30 seconds), crack one egg on top, break the yolk, and spread it evenly over the crepe. Sprinkle with scallions and sesame seeds.
7. Once the egg is set (about 1 minute), flip the crepe so the egg side is down. Cook for 20 to 30 seconds, then flip back.
8. Brush the crepe with about 1 tablespoon of the sauce mixture and chili sauce if you like.
9. Lay one youtiao (or cracker) across the center, fold the two sides over it, then fold in half. Serve right away while the filling is still crisp.
10. Repeat steps 4 to 9 with the remaining batter and eggs.

### Notes:

- **Flour:** Mung bean flour is what makes it Tianjin style, softer and slightly nutty. If you can't find it, use all wheat flour; it still works but tastes more like the versions sold outside Tianjin.
- **Guozi vs. guobizi:** In Tianjin you can choose either a youtiao or a *guobizi* (a thin, crispy fried sheet of dough). Fried wonton crackers are the closest easy substitute for guobizi.
- **Keep it simple:** The Tianjin version usually skips extras like lettuce, ham, or sausage, which you'll often see in jianbing sold in other cities.
- Where to buy: mung bean flour, sweet bean sauce, fermented bean curd, and frozen youtiao are all at Asian grocery stores such as 99 Ranch.
