# Chinese Hot Pot (火锅)

## Description

A pot of simmering broth sits in the middle of the table, and everyone cooks their own meat, vegetables, and noodles in it, then dips each bite in a sauce they mix themselves. Hot pot is eaten all over China, and the numbing, spicy *mala* style from Chongqing and Sichuan is the best known. This version uses a divided "yuanyang" pot (鸳鸯锅) with one spicy side and one mild side, so the whole group can share one pot. ([Wikipedia: Hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_pot))

### Ingredients:

**Broth**
- [ ] 1 package (about 7 oz / 200 g) store-bought spicy hot pot soup base, such as Little Sheep or Haidilao
- [ ] 1 package (about 4 oz / 110 g) mild soup base (bone broth or mushroom), or 6 cups (1.4 L) chicken stock
- [ ] 12 cups (about 3 L) water, divided, plus more to top up while eating
- [ ] 4 slices fresh ginger
- [ ] 3 scallions, cut into 2-inch pieces
- [ ] 6 dried red jujubes (optional, for the mild side)

**Meat and seafood**
- [ ] 1 1/2 lb (680 g) thinly sliced beef (frozen hot pot rolls)
- [ ] 1 lb (450 g) thinly sliced lamb or pork belly (frozen hot pot rolls)
- [ ] 1/2 lb (225 g) peeled shrimp
- [ ] 1 package (about 14 oz / 400 g) fish balls or beef balls

**Vegetables, tofu, and noodles**
- [ ] 1 small head napa cabbage, cut into large pieces
- [ ] 1 bunch spinach or tong ho (crown daisy), washed
- [ ] 7 oz (200 g) enoki mushrooms, root end trimmed
- [ ] 8 fresh shiitake mushrooms, stems removed
- [ ] 1 lb (450 g) daikon radish or 2 ears corn, cut into chunks
- [ ] 1 block (14 oz / 400 g) firm tofu, cut into 1-inch cubes
- [ ] 1 package fried tofu puffs (optional)
- [ ] 6 oz (170 g) sweet potato glass noodles, soaked in warm water for 30 minutes
- [ ] 2 packs fresh udon noodles, to finish the meal

**Dipping sauce bar** (each person mixes their own)
- [ ] 1/2 cup sesame paste (zhimajiang), thinned with warm water
- [ ] 1/2 cup soy sauce
- [ ] 1/4 cup Chinkiang black vinegar
- [ ] 1/4 cup toasted sesame oil
- [ ] 1 head garlic, minced
- [ ] 1 bunch cilantro, chopped
- [ ] 3 scallions, finely chopped
- [ ] Chili oil and Sichuan peppercorn oil, to taste
- [ ] Sugar and salt, to taste

### Steps for preparation:

1. Move the frozen meat to the fridge a few hours before dinner so it softens enough to separate, but keep it cold until you serve it.
2. Wash and cut all the vegetables, cube the tofu, and soak the glass noodles. Arrange everything on platters so people can reach them from their seats.
3. Set out the dipping sauce ingredients in small bowls with spoons, and give each person an empty bowl for mixing their own sauce. A good starting mix is 2 tbsp sesame paste, 1 tsp soy sauce, 1 tsp black vinegar, 1 tsp minced garlic, a pinch of sugar, and cilantro and scallions on top.
4. Place a portable induction or gas burner in the center of the table and set the yuanyang pot on it. If you don't have a divided pot, use two pots on two burners.
5. Spicy side: add the spicy soup base and 6 cups (1.4 L) water. Mild side: add the mild soup base and 6 cups (1.4 L) water (or use the chicken stock instead), plus the ginger, scallions, and jujubes.
6. Bring both sides to a boil over high heat, then lower the heat so the broth stays at a steady simmer.
7. Start with the ingredients that take longest: daikon or corn, tofu, fish balls, and shiitake mushrooms. Let them cook for about 10 minutes.
8. Everyone cooks their own meat: hold a slice in the broth with chopsticks or a small strainer ladle for 15 to 30 seconds, until it changes color, then dip it in sauce and eat. Shrimp need about 2 minutes, until they turn pink.
9. Add leafy greens, enoki, and glass noodles in small batches as you go. Greens need about 1 minute.
10. Skim off foam with a ladle now and then, and top up each side with hot water when the broth runs low.
11. When everyone is nearly full, cook the udon in the mild side for 2 to 3 minutes to finish the meal.
12. Turn off the burner before clearing the table, and let the pot cool before moving it.

### Notes:

- **No divided pot?** Two regular pots on two burners work fine. You can also make the whole pot mild and put chili oil on the sauce bar for the people who want heat.
- **Food safety:** Use separate chopsticks or ladles for raw meat and for eating, and make sure meat is fully cooked before eating it.
- **Scaling:** Plan on about 1/2 lb (225 g) meat and seafood per person, plus plenty of vegetables. It's easy to add more plates if people are still hungry.
- **Where to buy:** Soup bases, frozen sliced hot pot meat, fish balls, sesame paste, and glass noodles are all at Asian grocery stores such as 99 Ranch.
