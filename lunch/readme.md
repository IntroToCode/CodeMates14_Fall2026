# Cantonese Claypot Rice with Chinese Sausage and Shiitake (煲仔饭)

## Description
A Cantonese one-pot rice dish that steams jasmine rice with Chinese sausage, shiitake mushrooms, and ginger, then finishes with a savory soy drizzle and a crisp rice crust. Served with blanched choy sum, a tender-crisp Chinese green.

### Ingredients:
- [ ] 4 cups (about 800 g) jasmine rice, rinsed, soaked for 20 minutes, and drained
- [ ] 4 1/2 cups (1.06 L) water
- [ ] 4 teaspoons (20 mL) neutral oil, for the pots
- [ ] 2 tablespoons (30 mL) neutral oil, for the rice crust
- [ ] 6–8 Chinese sausages (lap cheong; about 450 g), thinly sliced on the diagonal
- [ ] 10 ounces (280 g) fresh shiitake mushrooms, stems removed and caps sliced
- [ ] 2 tablespoons (about 20 g) fresh ginger, thinly sliced
- [ ] 1/4 cup (60 mL) light soy sauce
- [ ] 2 tablespoons (30 mL) oyster sauce (or vegetarian mushroom sauce)
- [ ] 2 teaspoons (10 mL) dark soy sauce
- [ ] 2 teaspoons (10 mL) toasted sesame oil
- [ ] 2 teaspoons (8 g) sugar
- [ ] 1/4 cup (60 mL) water
- [ ] 4 scallions, thinly sliced
- [ ] 12 ounces (340 g) choy sum, rinsed and trimmed

### Steps for preparation:

1. Rinse the rice until the water is mostly clear. Soak it in fresh water for 20 minutes, then drain well.
2. In a small bowl, stir together the light soy sauce, oyster or mushroom sauce, dark soy sauce, sesame oil, sugar, and 1/4 cup water. Set aside.
3. Use two 2.5–3-quart (2.4–2.8 L) clay pots, or cook in two batches if you only have one. To each pot, add half of the drained rice, 2 1/4 cups fresh water, and 2 teaspoons neutral oil. Cover and bring to a gentle boil over medium-low heat. Reduce the heat to low and cook until most of the water is absorbed and the surface looks nearly dry, about 10–12 minutes.
4. Divide the sausage, mushrooms, and ginger between the pots and arrange them over the rice. Cover and cook on low for 10–12 minutes, until the rice is tender and the mushrooms are cooked through. If a pot becomes dry before the rice is tender, carefully add 1–2 tablespoons hot water around the edge.
5. Drizzle 1 tablespoon neutral oil around the inside edge of each pot. Cover and cook over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, carefully rotating each pot with heatproof mitts, until you hear crackling and a crisp crust forms on the bottom.
6. Turn off the heat and let the covered pots rest for 10 minutes.
7. While the rice rests, bring a separate pot of water to a boil. Blanch the choy sum for 1–2 minutes, until bright green and tender-crisp, then drain.
8. Drizzle the sauce over the rice in both pots and scatter the scallions on top.
9. Serve hot from the pots, scraping up some of the browned rice crust with each portion, with the blanched choy sum alongside.

### Notes:

- Traditional clay pots generally are not induction-compatible. For an induction stove, use an induction-compatible heavy-bottomed pot with a lid.
- For a vegetarian version, omit the Chinese sausage, use vegetarian mushroom sauce, and add browned firm tofu.
- Contains pork, soy, and sesame; soy sauces commonly contain wheat, and oyster sauce contains shellfish. Check package labels for exact allergens.
